# CS-255 Systems Analysis and Design: DriverPass

## Project Overview

DriverPass was a systems analysis and design project focused on helping a driving-training business improve the way it provides services to customers.

The client was **DriverPass**, a driving-training company that wanted an online system to help students prepare for their driving tests. The proposed system would allow customers to create accounts, purchase training packages, schedule driving lessons, take practice exams, and track their progress. It would also support administrative tasks, instructor scheduling, and the management of customer and lesson information.

The goal was to provide customers with convenient access to driving education while helping DriverPass manage its services more efficiently.

## What I Did Particularly Well

One area I did particularly well was connecting the client's business goals to practical system features. I considered the needs of different users, including customers, driving instructors, administrative staff, and management.

I also worked to organize the system requirements and design documents so that developers and stakeholders could understand how the proposed system should function. Using UML diagrams helped me translate written requirements into visual representations of system workflows, interactions, and relationships.

## What I Would Revise

If I could revise one part of my work, I would review the UML diagrams to ensure that every workflow, decision, and relationship was as clear and consistent as possible.

I would verify that each diagram matched the written requirements, use consistent terminology, and add clarifying notes where necessary. I would also seek feedback from stakeholders or classmates before finalizing the design. This would help identify confusing areas early and reduce the possibility of misunderstandings during development.

## Understanding and Implementing User Needs

I interpreted the users' needs by reviewing the client interview and business requirements, then identifying what each group needed to accomplish.

For example, customers needed a straightforward way to purchase packages, access practice exams, and schedule lessons. Instructors needed accurate lesson schedules, while administrative employees needed ways to manage customer and scheduling information. These needs helped guide the proposed features, workflows, and system models.

Considering user needs is important because a system can function correctly from a technical perspective but still fail if it does not solve the users' actual problems. Understanding users helps developers prioritize useful features, improve usability, reduce confusion, and ensure that the finished system supports the organization's goals.

## My Approach to Software Design

My approach to software design is to understand the problem before deciding on a solution. I would begin by reviewing stakeholder interviews, business requirements, assumptions, and project constraints.

Next, I would identify system users and use cases, distinguish functional requirements from nonfunctional requirements, and model important workflows and data relationships. I would use UML activity, sequence, use case, and class diagrams as appropriate to communicate different aspects of the system.

In future projects, I would validate requirements with stakeholders early, maintain traceability between requirements and design decisions, and use feedback to improve the models before development begins. I would also consider security, privacy, accessibility, error handling, and maintainability throughout the design process.

Finally, I would document assumptions and decisions clearly so developers, testers, and future maintenance teams could share a consistent understanding of the system.
